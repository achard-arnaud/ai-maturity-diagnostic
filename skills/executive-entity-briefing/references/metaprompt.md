# Metaprompt — canonical company × product × person two-pager

## Goal

Generate a reusable executive briefing without letting strong prose hide missing entity coverage.

Do not draft the final narrative first. Build and validate the atoms first, then assemble.

## Inputs

- company;
- person;
- purpose;
- audience persona: `francois-pro | sarah-pro | neutral`;
- geography;
- cutoff date;
- optional lead/message/context.

## Phase A — resolve entities

1. Confirm that the company and person refer to the intended entities.
2. Separate observed fact, inference, hypothesis and unknown.
3. Keep event date, publication date, observation date and cutoff date distinct.
4. If identity remains ambiguous, state the ambiguity instead of merging profiles.

## Phase B — company atom

Capture only decision-relevant fields:

- what the company does;
- business model and target clients;
- scale/footprint when evidenced;
- strategic trajectory and current momentum;
- differentiators and capabilities;
- named material products/frameworks/methodologies;
- proof points, contradictions and unknowns.

Avoid initiative catalogues. Explain the mechanism behind the positioning.

## Phase C — external-product atoms

Scan the evidence for named:

- products;
- platforms;
- services;
- frameworks;
- methodologies;
- diagnostics;
- programs;
- proprietary intellectual assets.

Assign each `core | material | supporting | incidental`.

For every `core` or `material` item create a dedicated atom containing:

1. what it is;
2. the problem it addresses;
3. its promise;
4. its mechanism or operating logic;
5. important components or stages;
6. its role in company positioning/business model;
7. target users/buyers when evidenced;
8. proof or adoption status;
9. differentiation versus obvious alternatives;
10. limitations, unknowns and falsifiers.

A named methodology cannot be reduced to a passing sentence when it materially explains the company.

## Phase D — person atom

Capture:

- current role and role-currency confidence;
- career spine rather than exhaustive CV;
- expertise;
- public theses and recurring themes;
- observable role in the company's positioning;
- geography/network context when relevant;
- contradictions and unknowns.

Do not infer authority, budget ownership or internal reporting lines from a title alone.

## Phase E — completeness gate

Before assembly, verify:

- company atom exists;
- person atom exists;
- every material product has its own atom;
- all material claims have source IDs;
- contradictions and unknowns are retained;
- no target product has been confused with our own `product_catalog`.

If a material product atom is absent, return to research/synthesis.

## Phase F — deterministic two-page assembly

### Page 1 — Who are they?

1. Executive snapshot: five to eight lines.
2. Company: activity, business model, footprint and trajectory.
3. Person: trajectory, expertise and current thesis.
4. Strategic intersection: why this person/company combination is distinctive.

### Page 2 — What matters for the conversation?

1. **Product spotlight(s), mandatory for every `core|material` product.**
   - what it is;
   - problem/promise;
   - mechanism/components;
   - role in positioning;
   - proof status;
   - differentiation;
   - limitations/unknowns.
2. Current dynamics and market motion.
3. Strengths/differentiators.
4. Points to challenge / proof gaps.
5. Audience-specific relevance and discussion angles.
6. One-sentence takeaway.

For a Rivard/Gabriel Greenfield-like case, a material methodology such as Meridian belongs here as a first-class product spotlight rather than being left only in the company narrative.

## Phase G — audience fit

Only after the three canonical atom families are frozen, derive:

- convergences with the audience;
- complementary strengths;
- useful disagreements;
- discussion questions;
- why the meeting may matter.

Keep these fields in the brief assembly. Never write them back into company/product/person atoms.

## Final QA

Reject the output if:

- page 2 contains no product spotlight despite a material named product;
- the person section is a CV dump;
- the company section is a list of announcements;
- unsupported size/revenue/adoption metrics appear;
- a title is presented as proof of power;
- audience fit is presented as target-company fact;
- a visual/page constraint caused evidence or a material product to disappear.
